      Cards/Bar Chart

      Bar Chart

      11 cardsHeight 2 units

      Bars start at zero, stay no thicker than 24px and are rounded only at the end. Vertical bars are for trends and comparisons; horizontal bars are for rankings and lists.

      Chart guide · Bar chart

      Vertical bars

      Trends and comparisons · 5 cards

      Bar - Basic

      bar-basic

      A vertical bar card that starts at zero. Use it to compare sizes across periods or items by length.

      Judgment built in (3)
      • Bars are read by length, so the vertical axis starts at zero. To follow the course of small changes, use a line card.
      • Bars are no thicker than 24px and the leftover width is left as space. Only the end is rounded; the base is flat, keeping the look of bars growing from the zero line.
      • The value is written only for the last bar, to the right of its end. A value on top of a narrow bar overlaps its neighbors. The other values are read by hovering or with the arrow keys.

      Bar - Stacked

      bar-stacked

      A card that splits each bar into stacked segments per item. Use it to see the course of a total and its composition together.

      Judgment built in (3)
      • The largest item goes at the bottom. Only a segment that starts at the zero line compares directly by length, so the most important item's course can be read.
      • Only the three largest items get a color; the rest fold into a gray Other. The rule matches KPI - Stacked Bar, so the same item gets the same color.
      • Segments are 2px apart so the colors do not blend, and the total is written to the right of the last bar and in the tooltip. If the data already has an "Other" series, mark it "other": true so it goes into Other.

      Bar - Stacked 100%

      bar-stacked-100

      A stacked bar card with every period scaled to 100%. Use it to see how the mix changes rather than the amounts.

      Judgment built in (3)
      • Every bar is scaled to 100%, so only the change in shares shows even as the total rises and falls. To see the change in the total as well, use Bar - Stacked.
      • The largest item goes at the bottom. A bottom segment that starts at the zero line shows the rise and fall of its share directly.
      • The tooltip gives both the share and the amount, to tell whether a share grew because that item grew or because another shrank. If the data already has an "Other" series, mark it "other": true so it goes into Other.

      Bar - Target

      bar-target

      A card that marks each bar's target with a short horizontal line. Use it to see whether each period met a target that differs by period.

      Judgment built in (3)
      • The target is a short horizontal line over the bar. Even when the target differs by period, the bar and its target are compared in the same place.
      • Periods that missed their target get a lighter bar, so attainment is read first. For a metric where lower is better, flip it with "better": "down".
      • The tooltip gives the target and the attainment rate together. The legend names are set with targetLabel (Target when empty) and valueLabel (Actual when empty).

      Bar - Diverging

      bar-diverging

      A card whose bars split above and below the zero line. Use it for signed values such as variance from target or change.

      Judgment built in (3)
      • Only the zero line is solid, and bars grow up and down from it. Where the sign flips is read first.
      • The good side is green and the bad side red, the same colors as the KPI badge. For a metric where lower is better, flip the colors with "better": "down".
      • Values are written with their sign, and the last bar's value sits to the right of its end.

      Horizontal bars

      Rankings and lists · 6 cards

      Bar - Ranking

      bar-ranking

      Horizontal bars with the name laid over the bar and the value on the right. Use it to see each item's size together with its rank.

      Judgment built in (3)
      • Items are sorted from largest to smallest. Items with a set order, like age bands or ranges, keep the written order with "sort": false.
      • Up to five rows are shown and the rest fold into one 'Other (n)' row. Other has no bar, only its total, so the folded sum does not blur the ranking. Change the row count with max.
      • The name is laid over the bar, so the bars use the full width with no name column. The tooltip gives the share of the total. A ratio metric (pct) cannot be added up, so it gets no share and the Other row gets no value.

      Bar - Compare

      bar-compare

      A card with a vertical tick for the comparison value over each horizontal bar and a change badge on the right. Use it to compare each item's value with last year or last month.

      Judgment built in (3)
      • The comparison value is a vertical tick, not a second bar. Whether the bar's end passed the tick tells at once whether the item grew or shrank.
      • The change badge is the same as the KPI cards': the arrow shows the direction and the color shows good or bad (better).
      • Sorting is by the current period's value. The folded Other row also gets a change, computed from the two totals. When a short bar puts the comparison tick over the name, the tick's middle is left open so it does not cross the letters. Up to five rows are shown and the rest fold into one Other row; change the count with max. The two period names in the legend are currentLabel and compareLabel.

      Bar - Butterfly

      bar-butterfly

      A card with the item names in the middle and the two groups' bars spread left and right. Use it to compare two groups, like men and women or two regions, on the same items.

      Chart guide · Butterfly chart ↗
      Judgment built in (3)
      • Both groups share one scale. The bars on both sides are sized to the single largest value, so left and right lengths compare directly.
      • Each item name is written once in the middle and the values sit in the outer cells. Ordered items like age bands keep the written order; "sort": true sorts by the sum of the two values.
      • The two groups are orange and pink-red, with each group's name at the top end of its bars. The tooltip gives both values, their total and the right-hand group's share.

      Bar Race

      bar-race

      A bar chart race: bars re-rank every period and overtake each other, showing how the order changed over time. Use it when the race itself is the story, in a presentation or a report. To compare values period by period, use Bump Chart or Line - Multiple.

      Judgment built in (3)
      • Give only the values: the card fills in between periods and re-sorts the bars by size at every moment. When one item overtakes another, the two bars swap places, so you see when the order changed. Bar length is relative to the current leader, which always spans the width, so read it as the gap within the ranking.
      • Only the focus item gets the first color; the rest are gray. The color follows the item, not the rank, so the eye can track one bar to the end. Leave it out and the card picks the item that climbed the most. Only the top max (8 by default) are shown; items that fall out slide down and fade.
      • The race plays once when the card comes into view and stops on the last period, because constant motion makes the cards next to it hard to read. Replay it with the button and pick a period with the slider; each period takes 0.9 s (step, in milliseconds). With reduced motion turned on, it does not play and shows the last period.

      Budget Bars

      bar-budget

      Each item gets a tick at its limit and a bar for the amount used so far, and only the part over the limit turns red. Use it for values with a limit they should not cross, such as budgets, headcount or capacity. For targets where more is better, use Bar - Target.

      Judgment built in (4)
      • Give each of the items a value (the amount used so far) and a limit. The limit is a vertical tick longer than the bar, named once above the first row by limitLabel (Limit when empty). Even when every item has a different limit, the end of the bar and the tick sit on the same row to compare.
      • The bar is gray within the limit and red only past it. Rows over the limit get a bold name and value, and the line below gives how many items went over and the total excess, computed by the card. Keeping color for the part over the limit makes where and by how much come first. For a rate (pct) the total is left out.
      • Use it only for limits that should not be crossed. On a target where more is better, the items that met it would blink red like a warning.
      • When the card comes into view the ticks stand first, the bars fill one at a time, then only the parts over the limit blink three times and stop. No bar outline is drawn before a bar fills. The still frame alone shows which items went over and by how much. Replay it with the button; with reduced motion turned on, the last frame is drawn right away. Items are drawn in the order given, up to 6 (max).

      Bar - Sort

      bar-sort

      Re-sorts the items by size and marks one of them, answering who is highest or lowest. Use it when an AI is asked "Who did best?" or "Who is furthest behind?" and has to show the answer. For a screen that only needs to show a ranking quietly, use Bar - Ranking.

      Judgment built in (6)
      • Write the kind of question in ask. With highest the card finds the item with the largest value itself, with lowest the smallest, and marks it in the first color. Because you do not name the item, the answer cannot disagree with the data. For metrics where lower is better, such as defect or churn rate, the best performer is lowest.
      • What to measure by is the decision of whoever composes the screen. "Did well" and "behind" give different answers by metric (sales, target attainment, growth), so write the metric and period you chose in subtitle, where the reader can check the basis. The card takes one value per item, so for a metric computed from two values, such as attainment or growth, put the computed value in value and the formula (sales ÷ target) in the subtitle.
      • Write an item name in focus only to mark an item that a calculation cannot decide, and leave ask out then (with both, focus wins). If the name is not in items, the card shows an error instead of drawing, so a wrong name never marks the wrong bar.
      • The rank (Highest of 8) and the gap to the average are computed by the card and written on one line under the chart, with the average drawn as a dotted line. For a rate (pct) the gap is in percentage points. Do not write these numbers (the place, the gap to the average) in the JSON or the title: when the data changes, the title alone would stay wrong. If title states a conclusion, it has to name the item the card marks.
      • Write items in the order you received them. The card shows that order first, then sorts, marks one item and writes the answer, about 2.5 seconds in all. Only one thing moves at a time, so the eye can follow what changes. The other items stay in gray, so the reader can check the answer. Values that can be negative, such as growth, are written as they are and drawn to the left of a zero line. From the ninth item on, only the 8 (max) nearest the marked item remain after sorting.
      • The card plays once when it comes into view and stops on the last scene, where the answer is written. That still frame alone shows the answer and its evidence, so a screenshot keeps the meaning. Replay it with the button; with reduced motion turned on, it does not move and shows the last scene.
